# The brief

One document, written for one reader, that says what the person wants. The reader
was not in the interview and should be able to act on the brief without coming back
to ask what was meant.

## Sections

Use these in this order. Rename the headings to suit the reader, the topic and the
language; keep the order and the meaning.

| # | Section | Required | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Header | yes | Who this document is for and what they should do with it |
| 2 | In one sentence | yes | What will exist or happen once it is done — not what kind of document or page carries it |
| 3 | Why | yes | What problem it solves, and who the end result is for |
| 4 | What it is | yes | The substance and every detail the person agreed |
| 5 | Out of scope | if discussed | What the person explicitly does not want |
| 6 | How we'll know it worked | if discussed | What the person will look at to call it a success |
| 7 | Open questions | yes | What is still undecided; "None" if nothing is |

Ask about sections 5 and 6 when the reader will clearly need them (a contractor
needs to know what is out of scope), not to fill the template.

## Writing

- **The person's substance, the reader's words.** Keep their meaning, numbers,
  names and terms; drop filler, repetition and the order in which things came up.
- **Plain words.** A term the reader may not know gets a short explanation the
  first time, or is replaced.
- **Decisions as decisions.** State what the person decided plainly, without
  hedging.
- **Open stays open.** Anything the person left undecided, unsure or "until I say"
  goes in section 7 only — never in section 4 or 5 as a decision or an exclusion.
- **Open questions for the reader.** Each one says what is not yet settled and what
  in the brief it touches, so the reader knows what to wait for; add who settles it
  where the log says so.
- **Structure that matches the substance.** A numbered list only for steps that
  really follow one another; a branch or a condition is written as one ("if…, then…").
- **Clean for the reader.** Entry numbers and "as you said" belong to the log; the
  brief reads as a document in its own right.
- **Layout:** one topic per paragraph; a list where items are parallel; a table only
  where the reader compares.

language.
